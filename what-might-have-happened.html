What Might Have Happened?

Modal Verbs for Past Deduction & Phrasal Verbs with "Out"

Goal Settings

By the end of this lesson, students can make natural deductions about past events, express different levels of certainty, and discuss evidence using authentic spoken English.

Warm-up

  • Have you ever misunderstood a situation?
  • Have you ever seen something strange and tried to explain it?
  • Do you enjoy mystery stories or true-crime documentaries?
  • What is the last surprising thing you found out?

Need Words?

Useful phrases for this lesson

  • She might have missed the train.
  • They could have gotten lost.
  • That can't have happened.
  • We need to figure it out.

Controlled Practice

1. Match evidence with deductions

His car wasn't in the driveway.
The lights were still on at midnight.
The door was locked from the inside.

2. Choose the strongest conclusion

Evidence: The package was wet, and it had been raining all night.

3. Complete the mini-dialogues

A: How did they ______ the truth?

B: I don't know, but it will ______ eventually.

Reading: The Missing Suitcase

Emma: Did you hear about the missing suitcase?
Ryan: Yeah. Someone must have taken it by mistake.
Emma: Maybe. But airport staff found it two hours later.
Ryan: Then the passenger might have left it at security.
Emma: That sounds more likely.
Ryan: It can't have been stolen if they found it inside the terminal.

Comprehension

1. What happened?
2. What is Ryan's first theory?
3. What evidence changes the conclusion?
4. Why does Ryan reject one possibility?
5. Which explanation seems most likely?

Native speakers use these forms to guess about the past when they don't know exactly what happened.

CertaintyExample
100% certainI know.
Very strong deductionHe must have forgotten.
PossibleShe might have missed the email. / They could have gotten lost.
ImpossibleThat can't have happened.

Listening: A Missed Meeting

A Missed Meeting at Work

0:00

(Audio file not bundled — play button simulates lesson timing. Use transcript below to follow along.)

A: Daniel wasn't at the meeting this morning.
B: Really? He must have forgotten about it.
A: Maybe, but he never forgets meetings.
B: Then he might have had a problem on the way to work.
A: That's possible. His train was delayed yesterday too.
B: Yeah, that could have happened.

Comprehension

1. Why wasn't Daniel at the meeting (according to speaker B)?
2. Why does speaker A doubt the first theory?
3. What is the second theory?
4. What past evidence supports the second theory?
5. What modal verb shows speaker B agrees with the new theory?

Post-listening Discussion

What explanations do people usually give for being late?
Have you ever made an incorrect assumption about someone?

Writing: A Strange Situation

Write 150–200 words describing an unusual event and present at least three possible explanations using modal verbs for deduction.
